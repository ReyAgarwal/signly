Quick start

  1. Allow the camera. When your browser asks, click Allow. The video never leaves this laptop.
  2. Get in position. Sit about an arm's length away. Hold your hand up by your shoulder, palm toward the camera.
  3. Copy the picture. Follow the numbered steps and the diagram (the tags name each finger).
  4. Hold still. The green bar fills while you hold the right sign. A ✓ means you got it.
  5. Fix the red finger. If a finger turns red, the hint above the bar says what to change.

Left-handed? Click Left at the top: every picture flips. Want photos of real signers too? Turn Photos on.

      How accurate is the coach?

      A fair test: the coach hides all the signs of one person, then tries to recognize them using only the other people's hands. It does this for every person, so the score shows how well it works for someone it has never seen, like a new user.

      When you sign a letter correctly, the coach can save that sign so it gets used to your hand and camera. To make sure it never learns a mistake, it only saves a sign when the real signers alone strongly agree it's correct (4 of the 5 closest matches).

      Add Samples is for a fluent signer or ASL teacher to add extra correct examples. Learners never need it.

      Backup and share

      Added samples, learned samples, and your progress are saved in this browser only. Export them to a file for a backup, or Import a teammate's file (Import adds to what's already here).

      Where the real-signer data comes from

      The reference hands were made from photos in two public datasets, both shared under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0) license. We ran MediaPipe on the photos and kept only the 21 hand points of each one. The letter photos shown next to the diagrams (two per letter) come from the first dataset; we made them square (filling the edges with a blurred copy of the photo), and mirrored some so they match the right-hand diagrams. The Photos switch at the top hides them.

      • Sowrow, A. R. A., Iftekhar, M. I. A. S., Prova, S. I., & Huq, M. R. (2024). Images of American Sign Language (ASL) Alphabet Gestures (Version 2). Mendeley Data. doi:10.17632/48dg9vhmyk.2
      • Cabana Garceran del Vall, E. (2026). American Sign Language Image Dataset (fingerspelling original + keypoints + evaluation) (Version 1). Mendeley Data. doi:10.17632/97yd3b5f5h.1
