Learn to fingerspell in ASL

Hold your hand up to the webcam. The coach compares your hand with thousands of hands of real people signing, recognizes the letter, tells you which finger to fix, and tracks your progress. Everything runs on this laptop: no internet needed, and no video ever leaves the computer.

The ASL Alphabet

Each picture shows a right hand the way you'll see it in the mirror video. The colors match the live skeleton on the camera, so you can match each finger. Click a letter (or just type it) to practice it.

Add Samples (for teachers)

You don't need this screen to learn. The coach already knows every letter from thousands of hands of real signers. Use it only if you are a fluent signer or ASL teacher adding extra correct examples, because the coach will treat them as correct.

Pick a letter, make the sign, then press Record (or Space). The coach saves one snapshot of the hand every tenth of a second for 3 seconds.

A

Tips for good samples
  • Record about 30-60 samples per letter. More is better.
  • Move your hand a little while recording: slightly closer, farther, and tilted.
  • Have two or more teammates record, and try both hands.
  • Use good lighting and keep your whole hand inside the video.

Test & Data

How accurate is the coach?

A fair test: the coach hides all the signs of one person, then tries to recognize them using only the other people's hands. It does this for every person, so the score shows how well it works for someone it has never seen, like a new user.

When you sign a letter correctly in Learn or Spell-a-Word, the coach can save that sign so it gets used to your hand and camera. To make sure it never learns a mistake, it only saves a sign when the real signers alone strongly agree it's correct (4 of the 5 closest matches).

Backup and share

Added samples, learned samples, and your progress are saved in this browser only. Export them to a file for a backup, or Import a teammate's file (Import adds to what's already here).

Where the real-signer data comes from

The reference hands were made from photos in two public datasets, both shared under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0) license. We ran MediaPipe on the photos and kept only the 21 hand points of each one; no photos are included in this app.

  • Sowrow, A. R. A., Iftekhar, M. I. A. S., Prova, S. I., & Huq, M. R. (2024). Images of American Sign Language (ASL) Alphabet Gestures (Version 2). Mendeley Data. doi:10.17632/48dg9vhmyk.2
  • Cabana Garceran del Vall, E. (2026). American Sign Language Image Dataset (fingerspelling original + keypoints + evaluation) (Version 1). Mendeley Data. doi:10.17632/97yd3b5f5h.1
